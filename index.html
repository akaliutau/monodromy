Founder story

From a neuroprocessor on FPGA at 17 to industrial robotics, frontier-model reasoning work, and now two AI product lines.

Aliaksei Kaliutau

Aliaksei’s path bridges frontier research and ambitious engineering. He first stood out at 17 by winning a national technology competition with a neuroprocessor designed on FPGA. With a foundation in Physics and Informatics and an MSc in AI from Imperial College London, he has worked on research software, large-scale AI and robotics infrastructure, and advanced reasoning and tool-use tasks for frontier models.

He founded Monodromy at I-X, Imperial College London, to turn that background into products: AIR for grounded autonomous software maintenance, and Aethelgard for decentralized clinical intelligence. Research remains deep, but the company is now organized around shipping systems, testing them against hard constraints, and building repeatable product infrastructure.
